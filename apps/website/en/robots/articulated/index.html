Research prototype data

Research specifications

The following data describe the six-legged research prototype reported in the paper.

Six-legged, six-bar-linkage research prototype
Mechanical designSix legs, each with a six-bar lifting mechanism
Drive configurationTwo bus-controlled servos per leg; 12 drives in total
Active degrees of freedomTwo per leg: horizontal swing and vertical lift
GaitsTripod and two-leg-support gaits
Peak speed, two-leg-support gait0.390 m/sMeasured on the research prototype
Average speed, two-leg-support gait0.174 m/sSame 20-second experiment

Test conditions: 20 seconds of forward travel on a level laboratory floor, with servo speed set to 0.2 revolutions per second. Speed was estimated by integrating acceleration from a nine-axis IMU. These are published research results, not delivery specifications. Dimensions, mass, payload, runtime and interfaces depend on the specific prototype and test results.

How it moves

Mechanism and gaits

From single-leg motion to coordinated multi-leg walking.

01

Independent swing and lift

One servo controls horizontal swing; the other drives vertical lift through a six-bar linkage. The lifting mechanism has one degree of freedom, making the relationship between input angle and foot trajectory easier to analyze.

02

Tripod gait

Two groups of three legs provide alternating support. Static stability is possible when the center-of-mass projection remains inside the support triangle, making this gait suitable for initial testing and support-timing research.

03

Two-leg-support gait

Three pairs of legs provide alternating support, requiring dynamic balance. The published experiment recorded higher speed and greater body vibration with this gait, providing a basis for studying speed and stability.

Preparing an experiment

Operating guidance

Guidance for introductory teaching and experimental preparation.

  1. Inspection and calibration

    Check power requirements, fasteners and wiring. Verify servo neutral positions and joint directions using the hardware instructions.

  2. Low-speed testing

    Start with the tripod gait on level, unobstructed ground. Check foot clearance, support sequence and motion limits.

  3. Records and maintenance

    Adjust gait parameters incrementally and record speed, posture and temperature rise. Stop and inspect if binding or abnormal vibration occurs.

Follow the hardware documentation for initial power-up, emergency stopping and software operation.
