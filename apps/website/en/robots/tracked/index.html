T1 Tracked inspection robot

T1 is a tracked inspection robot in development for mining. Research focuses on adapting a tracked platform to site conditions and integrating environmental sensing, with an enclosure designed around a tracked chassis.

Starting with site requirements

Research assesses tracked-platform requirements around mine passages, equipment layouts and observation points.

Tracked-platform adaptation

Develop the robot layout and enclosure around a tracked chassis, checking clearances, turning space and ground conditions along the intended route.

Environmental sensing

Assess cameras, LiDAR and other sensors against inspection targets, including sensor placement and retention of observation records.

Inspection workflows

Connect movement, observation and record keeping with maps, inspection points and task feedback, while validating OmindOS integration.

Mining fieldwork · Earlier research

Tracked-platform research underground

The team has conducted research in underground metal mines, including driving and environmental data capture with an earlier tracked platform. These field records inform T1 requirements and future validation.

The videos show an earlier research platform whose appearance and configuration differ from the current T1 enclosure concept. They do not establish T1 performance or certification.

Define configuration and validation around the task

Start with a single route and inspection point. Confirm the robot configuration, software version and evaluation method before expanding the test scope.

Site conditions

Check passage dimensions, slopes, obstacles, lighting and communications coverage. Define operator intervention and fault-handling procedures.

Hardware and software

Select sensors and onboard computing for the task, then confirm control interfaces and the scope of OmindOS integration and testing.

Test records

Link routes, inspection points, original imagery and operator interventions. Assess suitability using the specific configuration and recorded test results.

The published field records come from underground metal-mine research. Applications in potentially explosive atmospheres require separate review of the exact model, certification documentation and site access requirements.
