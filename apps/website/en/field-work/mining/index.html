Applications / Mining inspection

Mining inspection and robotics research

We are developing the Q1 quadruped platform and OmindOS together for underground inspection, drawing on metal mine field research into access, equipment inspection and review of results.

Define what to inspect and record

These tasks provide a starting point for research and joint validation. Each requires defined inspection targets, data collection methods and assessment criteria.

Records and review

Inspection records

Agree on timestamps, locations, equipment identifiers and original images. Route findings to people for review and define any connections to existing systems.

Operating conditions

Validate each environmental constraint

Mine type, operating area and site conditions determine the configuration. Low light, dust, moisture and limited communications each require defined tests and assessment methods.

GNSS-denied areas
Assess repetitive tunnels, map changes and recovery after localization loss.
Low light and occlusion
Review lighting, observation distance and sensor coverage. Retain original images.
Dust and moisture
Assess sensor contamination, enclosure protection, cleaning needs and ground traction.
Slopes and obstacles
Check gradients, steps, clearance, turning space and accessible routes under the intended payload.
Communications and power
Define communications coverage, response to connection loss, manual takeover and the task energy budget.
Hazardous conditions
Where combustible gases, explosive dust, high temperatures or corrosion are present, assess the complete robot and site access requirements separately.

Assess hazardous-area suitability separately

Our published material primarily covers underground metal mine research and demonstrations on several platforms. It does not establish that Q1 has explosion-protection or mining safety certification. Use in coal mines or other potentially explosive environments requires a separate review of the model, certification and site access requirements.

Research foundation / Kuanghai Mingmou

Connecting localization, perception and inspection

The Kuanghai Mingmou project studies semantic mining inspection across multiple platforms. Multi-sensor localization provides maps and position, 3D perception represents the environment, and task planning connects mobility with inspection actions.

01 / GNSS-denied environments

Multi-sensor localization

Research combines LiDAR, camera and inertial measurements for point-cloud registration, incremental mapping and trajectory estimation in mine tunnels.

02 / Incomplete observations

3D reconstruction and perception

Research explores neural radiance fields (NeRF) and geometric priors to reconstruct scenes from incomplete observations.

03 / Changing inspection tasks

Semantic understanding and planning

Language instructions and scene semantics help break inspection goals into steps that connect route planning, dynamic obstacle avoidance and task execution.

From a map to an inspection record

Associate inspection targets with map locations, organize movement, observation and recording, then assess how those functions work together along the same route.

Common questions

Does the underground footage show Q1?

The underground video shows a tracked platform. The point cloud comes from the Kuanghai Mingmou project. Both provide site and research context; see the Q1 product page for its development status and demonstrations.

What can we work on together now?

We can discuss inspection requirements, assess software integration for existing robots and design test tasks together. Q1 is at the engineering prototype stage, so the scope depends on prototype readiness and site conditions.

Can Q1 be used in coal mines or explosive environments?

A mining demonstration alone cannot establish suitability. The model, hazards, certification and site access requirements must be reviewed before considering a test. Metal mine recordings do not demonstrate explosion-protection compliance.
